arq radar

An organization's own instance on the arq decisions forecasting platform: a dynamic monitor that shows where things are moving on its questions about the future.

What it is

You don't reach a goal by aiming precisely once, but by steering continuously. With the Radar, people from across an organization and its environment assess its questions about the future on an ongoing basis, an AI forecast runs alongside, and arq decisions analyzes the results with the organization. It runs for one to three years, in cycles of four weeks to six months, and often carries on the questions from a Future Atlas made with Perspektiv. Each cycle brings in more people: departments, specialist groups, members or the whole workforce, chosen to fit the goal. Estimates are anonymous, and the reporting comes with reference points.

What came out of it

A dynamic monitor: the organization sees where estimates shift and which paths are emerging, and a critical threshold on each question shows when action is needed, while there is still time to steer. Knowledge spread across departments and sites flows into the same questions, and people raise topics and receive the insights, even without a seat at the strategy table. Over time the organization builds its own Center of Excellence and applies the method itself; arq decisions joins once per cycle for a check-in. Why one to three years: only after about 60 resolved questions does a track record show as much skill as luck, so the Radar runs long enough for an organization to learn whom it can trust. That figure comes from "Whom to Trust, and When?", with 5,898 forecasts by 177 people on the arq decisions forecasting platform.

Research

  • Whom to Trust, and When? Aggregating Probability Forecasts in Small Crowds with Short Track RecordsDraft, 2026. Should organizations weight forecasters by their track record or take the simple average? A model and 5,898 forecasts by 177 forecasters show that with small crowds and short records, the simple average is hard to beat.
