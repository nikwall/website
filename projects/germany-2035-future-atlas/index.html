Can Germany grow into its bill? Starting from the 2026/27 annual report of the German Council of Economic Experts: nine uncertainties, four scenarios for 2035, and 39 signals with base rates, the first resolving in December 2026.

What it is

Defence, interest and ageing largely fix Germany's bill until 2035. The German Council of Economic Experts calls closing the gap in the federal budget a consolidation task of historic scale (annual report 2026/27, published on 11 November 2026). What remains open is how harsh the environment becomes, and how broad the base that has to carry the bill.

Nine uncertainties, each with a criterion that can be checked later, a base rate from public data and our estimate, form two bundles that barely move together (correlation −0.04): the environment (defence, new crisis funds, energy prices) and the base (productivity, migration, social contributions, interest against growth). Whether the deficit is back at 3% of GDP by 2035 depends on both, so the map shows its probability in each of the four scenarios.

39 signals with a date and a base rate show early which way the country is heading; 19 of them resolve by June 2027. On the atlas, every signal can be set to yes or no to see where the marker moves. It is a public example of arq perspektiv, the method of arq decisions.

Where the map stands today

  • Most likely scenario: Double burden (Doppelte Last), 41%: a harsher environment and a narrower base. The assumptions of the annual report lie in this field.
  • The four scenarios: Broad growth 12%, Growth under pressure 31%, Quiet ageing 16%, Double burden 41%.
  • Deficit at or below 3% of GDP in 2035: 45% overall, from 29% under a double burden to 75% under broad growth.
  • First signals: AI adoption in 2026 (December), GDP and the deficit for 2026 (January), the gas price in January (February).
