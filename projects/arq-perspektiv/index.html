In two weeks, from many views to one shared picture: three workshops and an AI analysis that end in a Future Atlas.

What it is

A Perspektiv is a small hand telescope. Standing in confusing terrain, you quickly gain an overview with it: its lenses focus the light into one clear image, just as Perspektiv focuses many viewpoints into one shared view. It is the way in: three sessions, seven hours in all, with up to eight people from the organization and arq's analysis in between. In the foresight workshop the group works in four steps from a picture of the future, through a pre-mortem and a cone of possible futures, to the critical uncertainties. In the forecasting workshop these become forecasting questions, which the group assesses and gives a threshold. An AI then researches every question, with reasoning and sources. In the synthesis the group discusses this analysis and makes the results concrete.

What came out of it

After two weeks the organization has its Future Atlas: where it stands today, every path analyzed with reasoning and sources, and the paths into the future that are emerging and why. Inside are the key messages on one page, a map on which two open questions span four futures and a dot shows where the signals point today, all signals with the AI's estimate, the group's estimate and the threshold at which each becomes critical, and one page per question. It is a book to look things up in, not one to file away. Why up to eight people: five people reach 86% of the accuracy gain of a group of two dozen, and ten reach 95%, as long as each person first gives an estimate alone. With the Radar, the questions keep running.

Research

  • Whom to Trust, and When? Aggregating Probability Forecasts in Small Crowds with Short Track RecordsDraft, 2026. Should organizations weight forecasters by their track record or take the simple average? A model and 5,898 forecasts by 177 forecasters show that with small crowds and short records, the simple average is hard to beat.
